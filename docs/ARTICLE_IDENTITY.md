# Article identity

One article per canonical URL. The reader over a web page and the reader from the library show
the same article, with the same playhead and the same audio, whether or not it is saved; Save and
*Remove from Library* only flip the bookmark. (The reader screen itself: `NAVIGATION.md` → "The reader".)

## Rules (identity v2)

| Concern | Rule |
|---------|------|
| Canonical URL | Lowercased scheme and host, no fragment, default port dropped, tracking parameters removed (`utm_*`, `fbclid`, `gclid`, `mc_*`, `igshid`, `si`, …), trailing slash trimmed except at the root (`ArticleIdentity.canonicalURLString`). |
| Same page | Two addresses are the same page when their canonical URLs are equal (`URL.isSamePage`): ⋯ → Website shows the browser's page as it is, without a reload, when it is the article's page; History keeps one visit per page; the browser's live favicon counts for a page only while the browser shows it. |
| Article key | `ArticleIdentity.articleKey(canonicalURL:)` = SHA-256(namespace v2, canonical URL) → UUID. No content in the key. |
| Library page | `Page.canonicalURL`, one page per canonical URL app-wide, whatever its state (link, saved, or played without saving). `Page.key` is the audio cache key: pages made since identity v2 use the article key; pages copied from saves made before it keep that save's random id, copied verbatim at migration and never recomputed, and are found by canonical URL. |
| Open from a page | `LibraryStore.readerArticle`: the page for the canonical URL → its key, position and filled bookmark (if saved); else the URL key. A page holding text is refreshed when the listen text changed; a link stays a link. |
| Save | `LibraryStore.save` (through `ArticleSaveCoordinator`) is idempotent: the page for the canonical URL turns saved in place, keeping its folder; else a page is inserted with `key = reader key`. Once that is stored, the audio cache is marked saved and the queue job turns non-ephemeral (audio work recorded with the save, `plan(.saved, key:)`, so a launch that dies right after it has it done by the next one). The session and playhead are untouched, and saving the page that is playing, under its own key, moves no audio. When an older page holds the address under another key, Save moves the reader's audio under the page's key (`migrateListenCache`) and rekeys the live session to it (`rekeyActiveSession`), without restarting playback. A session on the reader's key still playing older text (bound before the page was refreshed on open) gives the page no playhead, and is stopped rather than rekeyed: no page holds what it plays. Saving a page that isn't playing, under its own key, aligns its audio with the saved text and adopts a legacy v1 cache into the key when the key has none (`prepareListenIdentity`, as on open). A save that can't be stored is rolled back and touches no audio. |
| Play without saving | The first play of a page the library doesn't have keeps it as a page not in the Library (`addedAt == nil`) with its text and position, pruned 7 days after it was last played. Playing a link in the Library keeps it a link and its text as a cache. |
| Remove from Library | One operation (`LibraryStore.remove`) from every path: a Library row's swipe or menu, Edit, 🔖 and the ⋯ of the browser, the Reader and Now Playing. The page leaves its folder and every list (`removedAt`; Favorites and "Not saved" too), kept 7 days after its last play for what plays and the bar (a page never played goes at the next launch). Its star stays, so saving it again puts it back in Favorites. Its audio stays, marked ephemeral once that is saved (pruned after 7 days or past 500 MB). Deleting a folder with its contents deletes its pages and their audio. |
| Content change | `ArticleIdentity.contentFingerprint(paragraphs:)`, over the listen blocks after anti-copy stripping, only detects change. |
| Per-paragraph reuse | Every cached paragraph stores `textHash` (`ArticleIdentity.paragraphHash`). On open, `ArticleAudioCache.reconcile` keeps matching units, moves shifted ones to their new index and drops edited ones, so only changed paragraphs re-render. |
| Older caches | v1 ephemeral dirs (URL + content key) are adopted into the v2 key on open when the v2 dir is empty; unhashed saved units are stamped with the saved copy's stored paragraphs. |
| Duplicates | `LibraryStore.dedupe` (launch, idempotent) merges pages that share a key or a canonical URL (keeper = saved over link, then furthest position, latest play, oldest), copying in what the keeper lacks (`Page.absorb`: content, address, the earliest add, the latest play, a spot, finished, starred and skipped if either was) and, once that is saved, merging the other's audio into the keeper, hash-reconciled (`LibraryAudioWork`). Logged as `[Library] dedupe page …`. |
| Migration | Saved articles (`SavedArticle`, frozen) were copied once into pages with the same key; a receipt saved with the copy records what was copied, so a page deleted later never comes back and rows made by an older build during a rollback are copied on the next launch. No audio moved. |

## Why content is not in the key

Pages differ from load to load even when the article doesn't: some sites randomize class names and
insert hidden anti-copy lines on every load. The visible-text snapshot (`ReaderVisibleSnapshot`)
strips most of that, but any leak would change a content-based key and re-render the whole
article. With the URL as the key and a hash per paragraph, a leak costs at most that one
paragraph. Re-extracting the article when the reader opens is expected (fast, local); it doesn't
re-render audio.

## Imported books

A book chapter has no web address; its key comes from the book (`BookIdentity`), so importing
the same file again gives the same keys, and its audio is reused.

| Concern | Rule |
|---------|------|
| Book key | SHA-256(namespace `book.v1`, `"identifier"`, the OPF unique identifier, the title) → UUID. The title is in it because editions share identifiers. An identifier that is missing or a placeholder of zeros gives `"file"` and the file's SHA-256 instead. Stored as the book folder's `sourceIdentifier`; one book folder per key. |
| Chapter key | SHA-256(namespace `book-chapter.v1`, the book key, the spine file's path, the anchor) → UUID, the anchor being the file's start, an id the book gives, or the first block's text and how many chapters before it in that file open the same way. Not through `canonicalURLString`, which drops fragments. `Page.key`; `Page.locator` keeps `href#fragment`. |
| Final keys | Chapters are settled on their listen blocks before any key is stored (`BookChapterFinalizer`): a chapter dropped or joined to a neighbour leaves no key behind; the chapter it joins keeps its own. |
| Importing a book already in the Library (D13) | *Cancel* changes nothing. *Replace* rebuilds the pages in the same folder, which keeps its place, its name, its order and its listening (`LibraryStore.replaceBook`). The book's chapters are the pages its record names (`BookSourceState.chapterKeys`), found by key wherever they were moved (D19): one moved out is updated where it is; one the file dropped is deleted only if it is still in the book; pages moved in from elsewhere are never touched; a new chapter goes into the book just after the chapter before it. A key match alone doesn't prove a chapter is the same text: a chapter whose key and listen text both match keeps everything; one whose key matches but text changed keeps its audio per paragraph (aligned by text hash) and its position only if the paragraph it is in is still there word for word, else it starts again; it is no longer finished (a finished one starts again from its top, so neither Continue nor the D7 trim treats the revision as heard), and playback of it stops; a chapter under a new key whose text matches an old page takes over that page's place, position, marks (finished, starred, skipped in playback) and audio (`Page.absorb` and an audio merge, as dedupe does), and a session playing it follows, unless a chapter with the old key is back by the time that audio work runs, which keeps that audio and its session as its own; while that work waits, a later *Replace* that revises or drops the chapter it merged into, or deleting that chapter, stops the session, as it stops that chapter's own. Under those rules sits one that holds whatever the order of later *Replace*s: listening is stored on a chapter only while it holds the text being played, and a session follows a merge only onto a chapter holding its text, else it stops (`LibraryStore.holds`). A session that plays on through a *Replace* giving its key to other text (a chapter brought back under it) records nothing on that chapter. The book's chapters no chapter comes from that are still in the book are deleted; their audio goes once the save succeeds (`LibraryAudioWork`), unless a chapter with its key is back by the time that work runs, which keeps it as its own audio. Replace is one edit: one that can't save leaves the book and its audio as they were, and the import sheet says so. The book's place follows its page; when that page is dropped, it moves to the nearest page before it in the book's order that stays, as deleting a page moves it (to none if no earlier page stays). |
