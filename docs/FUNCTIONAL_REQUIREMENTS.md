# Functional requirements

Meridian Reader is a personal tool for **listening to web articles**: find an article in the in-app
browser, get a clean copy, and listen to it (or read it) on the device, offline once saved. How
the screens look and move: `NAVIGATION.md`. How it is built: `TECHNICAL_SPEC.md`.

## Surfaces

| Surface | Role |
|--------|------|
| **Library** | The folder tree: the home is the top level, with Everything (its folders, books and series among them, then the pages in no folder, pages played without saving on top, in the top level's sort) and Favorites (starred folders and pages from anywhere) tabs, then folder → folder at any depth. No tab bar. On compact width the floating chrome host (`NAVIGATION.md`, "Bottom chrome"): the search field, then the pill `[Reader | Library | Browser]`, ▶ and ⋯ (＋'s rows in ⋯, History a tile of it). On regular width the same band as the browser (D22, D23): the search field, then ‹ › ＋ 🕘 ⋯ in the browser's ‹ › 🔖 🕘 ⋯ slots. |
| **Browser** | A start page (Favorites, Recent Searches) or a web page, with one bottom bar (address field + toolbar). |
| **Reader** | A sheet for reading any page, over the browser or the Library, with its own Listen. |
| **Now Playing** | A sheet over everything for what you hear, in the Reader's reading theme and type: a title card, the text following the audio (the spoken paragraph highlighted, every paragraph in full ink), and the player's controls. |
| **Settings** | ⋯ › Settings, the last row of every ⋯ panel (the sidebar's bottom row on iPad): Listening (voice, language, speed), Appearance (System / Light / Dark, five app colours) and Reading (theme, font, size, spacing, Scroll / Pages; the same as Aa) as pages, Browsing (Block Ads), Prepared Audio & Storage, About (Privacy Policy, Support, Acknowledgements), and Developer (developer options only). |
| **Now Playing bar** | What you hear, in the Reader's band while another page plays, from the first play until *Stop Listening* (long-press: the page's menu, then *Stop Listening*) or the page is removed from the Library (its menu's *Remove from Library*, or anywhere else), deleted, or revised by a book's *Replace*; it keeps a finished page with ▶. Tap or swipe up → Now Playing. Elsewhere on compact width the chrome's play/pause (with its progress ring) and the Reader segment do its job; on regular width the clustered dock does. |

On iPad (regular width) the surfaces sit beside a sidebar; see *iPad* below.

The app reopens on the surface you left: the Library at the folder you were in, or the browser on
its last article (reloaded). A fresh install opens the Library.

## Browser

| ID | Requirement |
|----|-------------|
| B1 | The address field takes a URL (loaded in the in-app web view) or search text (a Google search). |
| B2 | Pages open only by user action; links navigate normally. ‹ / › are web history. |
| B3 | The start page shows starred pages that have their own http(s) address as a Favorites icon grid (no folders here), along with Recent Searches; a tap opens that address in Browse; Show All opens the Library's Favorites tab (which still lists starred folders and every starred page). |
| B4 | While typing: local matches (history, saved articles) first, then live Google suggestions (unofficial endpoint, debounced). If Google fails, only local matches show, with no error. |
| B5 | History keeps the last 30 visited pages (title, host, favicon), deduped by canonical URL. Clearing history never deletes saved articles. |
| B6 | ☆ *Add to Favorites* sets an independent star flag without moving the article. A new favorite may be a link (URL, title, favicon) without being saved; an article stays where it is, in the Library or not (starring never files it). Favorites lists every starred article, in the Library or not, and a starred article is never pruned. Unstarring only clears the star. *Remove from Library* never clears it: the article leaves every Library list and stays in Favorites. One article per address; a chapter can be starred like any other article. |
| B7 | Dictation fills the address field while searching, on-device where the language allows; nothing is submitted automatically, and listening pauses while the mic is open. |
| B8 | Loading pages needs the network. |

DRM EPUBs, password-protected PDFs, scanned/image-only PDFs (no OCR), and non-http(s) shares are refused or ignored as today. Cold start and warm start both work. |
| B9 | A readable article shows 🎧 at the end of the address field: grey when it isn't what you hear (tap: listen now, replacing what plays once this article is extracted; "Nothing to listen to on this article" when it has too little text), accent with bars when it is (tap: pause / resume). |
| B10 | Video and audio on a page play only after a tap. *Block Ads* (Settings › Browsing, on by default) keeps requests to common ad and tracker hosts from loading, in every web view the app makes; a link through an ad host (a sponsored search result) doesn't open; a change applies to pages loaded after it. |

## Save and the Library

| ID | Requirement |
|----|-------------|
| S1 | Save is available from the browser (⋯ → *Save to Folder…*, into a folder picked in the Move tree) and the Reader, and always runs extraction. |
| S2 | On success the full article is stored on the device (text, cleaned HTML, metadata). |
| S3 | On failure Save is blocked with the reason, and nothing is stored. |
| S4 | One article per canonical URL: saving, adding to Favorites or listening to the same article twice (tracking parameters, fragments) keeps one article, in at most one folder. |
| S5 | Folders nest to any depth. A folder lists its subfolders, then its pages by its sort (Manual, Newest, Title); Edit selects rows to move, mark finished or remove, and drags them when Manual. Without Edit, a row lifted with a long press is filed by dropping it on a folder, or placed by dropping it between rows (which makes the folder's sort Manual); a folder can't go into itself or a folder inside it. Books and series are folders like any other: they take drops and subfolders, and their chapters move, drag and reorder as any page. Folders can be made, renamed, moved and deleted: deleting offers only *Delete Folder and Contents*, which deletes everything inside with its audio. A delete that can't be saved leaves the pages and their audio. The home's Favorites tab and search results gather rows from across the tree: they don't drag, and a folder there opens under its parents. |
| S6 | Rows show a length, "N min left" part-way or "Finished", with the listening mark in a right-aligned column before ⋯ (a ring part-way, the same ring full when finished, no ✓; bars while playing) (folders: what is in them, or their place; every length and time left in the app counts at the playback speed), a small accent filled ↓ when prepared by a prepare (C89, C97) (in the current voice, or one kept; listening alone never marks it), the prepare state in one place, the small mark before the second line (a spinner queued, the accent ↓ filling while it prepared audio, filled when done; the thumbnail never changes, C102), and the playing row is tinted. A tap plays a page row (Now Playing for what you hear, the browser for a link) and opens a folder row; the row's only control is ⋯, and a long-press opens the same menu: the item's one menu, the same rows in the same order wherever the page or folder shows (rows, Favorites tiles, History visits, the Now Playing bar, a folder's header and screen, the Reader, Now Playing, the browser; NAVIGATION.md → "Menus"); swipe left is its *Remove from Library* (*Remove from Recent*, a folder's *Delete Folder…*). Removing a page, from here or anywhere else (Edit, 🔖, the ⋯ of the browser, the Reader and Now Playing, the Now Playing bar's long-press), is one action: it takes the page out of every list it was in (Pages, its folder, search, Prepared Audio) and keeps its star (Favorites lists only saved pages, so it shows there again once saved, C104), and it doesn't come back in Recent (saving it, or listening to it again with 🎧 or the Reader's Listen, does); the page you hear stops, and the Now Playing bar offers the page played before it, if one is still listed; a remove that can't be saved changes nothing and says so. |
| S7 | Nothing on the Library home appears or goes away because playback starts or ends. The row of any folder whose pages play (a book or series too) shows its place (the page it is at) and time left ("6 h 10 min left"), and its ⋯ › *Continue* resumes it there without opening it; a part-way page's row shows the time left and a tap resumes it; the Now Playing bar's ▶ resumes what you last heard. Pages played without saving are in the home's Recent tab (not Everything), with *Save to Library* and *Remove from Recent* in their menu; search covers the tab on screen (on a folder's screen, everything under that folder). |
| S8 | A book or series is a folder: it opens to the folder screen with its details (cover, author), a Continue / Play from Start button, and its chapters, first in the source's order (front matter skipped in playback, *Skip in Playback* per chapter); after that its order, sort and contents are the user's, as for any folder. Playing any chapter carries on through the book in its order, then stops. |
| S11 | Any other folder with pages that play plays like a book: its screen has the same Continue / Play from Start button, and playing any of its pages carries on through its pages in its order (its sort), page to page, passing over link pages, then stops. The page that last played is its place. A page in no folder plays to its end and stops. Now Playing says what it plays from: the folder, else the Library or the site. |
| S9 | Search in the Library covers titles, sites, folder names and authors across the tree (begun on a folder's screen, under that folder, and the field names it), and offers *Search the web*. |
| S10 | *Import from…* (the Library's ＋ and ⋯, the empty Library) imports a DRM-free EPUB or a text PDF picked in Files, or an `.epub` link the browser downloads (asked first), as a book: one folder with one page per chapter (PDF: one chapter per text page; no OCR). EPUB front matter is skipped in playback. The import shows its progress, can be cancelled (keeping nothing), and files the book in *Add to* (the folder it started from, else the Library), which can change while it runs; it then opens the book. One import runs at a time. Copy-protected, password-protected, fixed-layout, too-large and unreadable files are refused with a one-line reason. A book already in the Library asks *Replace* (same folder, its order kept, places kept where chapters match; chapters moved elsewhere are found and updated where they are, new ones go after the chapter before them) or *Cancel*. |
| S12 | *Follow…* (the browser's ⋯ on a story's page, the Library's ⋯ with a typed address) reads the story's table of contents (Royal Road's story page, or any site's longest list of chapter links) into Review: every chapter with a box, *Select All*, and a preview of a chapter on tapping its row. OK makes the story's folder at once with the chapters kept, as links, whose text is then fetched while the app is open, strictly one at a time with a pause, backing off when the site asks to slow down or doesn't answer (a notice in the folder's header names the chapter that waits, by its title), with one status line in the folder's header, which also counts chapters that couldn't load. ▶ on a chapter still waiting fetches it first, then plays it; playing through the story stops before a chapter not fetched yet. About once a day while the app is in use the contents are read again and new chapters go at the end (left-out ones stay out). The Following sheet shows the last and next check and the fetch progress, with *Check Now* and *Unfollow* (the chapters stay). No feeds, no notifications. |

## Listening

| ID | Requirement |
|----|-------------|
| P1 | Phonon (on-device, English, Core ML) is the default voice on a supported device (Zoey), Kokoro (on-device, English, A15+; Heart) the second default, and Apple TTS is always available and the fallback. Each on-device engine downloads its model on selection (deletable for Kokoro voice packs) and is used only once ready. Apple speaks whenever the selected engine can't (not downloaded, loading, failed, another language, a chunk it can't render live), in the best installed Apple voice for the language (Premium, then Enhanced, then default; the engine voice's gender first). Phonon renders only with the app in front; Kokoro's ONNX route keeps rendering in the background. C129: a Kokoro crash never switches engine or voice: the same voice resumes on ONNX CPU silently (one thread fewer each time); after a second crash in a row downloads and new rendering pause, playback goes on through audio already rendered, and only where it reaches audio not rendered yet does it pause there with a plain banner and Retry (Retry restarts Kokoro and resumes rendering and downloads). Apple audio is never written to the download cache. An app install that cuts a call off isn't counted as a crash. |
| P2 | Playback never goes silent or freezes mid-article: anything the selected engine can't produce is spoken with Apple. |
| P3 | Each article keeps one voice's audio; switching voice re-renders it, and the old voice plays until each paragraph is replaced. |
| P4 | The position is remembered per article and resumed later, for an article listened to without saving too (kept while it is in the last 50 of Recent, C134); after a Kokoro crash the app reopens the article at the paragraph that was playing, or at its own spot, paused, when its text has changed since. |
| P5 | Listening continues in the background, with lock-screen / Control Center controls (play, pause, next / previous paragraph, rate). Calls and unplugged headphones pause; a resumable interruption resumes. |
| P6 | The article's language is detected; an article the selected engine can't speak uses an Apple voice for its language, without changing settings. |
| P7 | Saved articles play fully offline. |
| P8 | *Download* on a page, folder, book or followed story fetches its text (keeping a link page's in place) and renders its whole audio in the on-device voice ahead of time, one page at a time, behind what is playing, while the app is in front and, on the ONNX CPU route, in the background while audio plays (the playing page always first). A folder's Download is a toggle (its header's Download control, and one *Download* / *Turn Off Download* row in its menus): while on, every page added to it later downloads too; turned off, what is downloaded stays; turned back on over a folder all downloaded, nothing is downloaded again. A slim line on the bottom chrome shows progress while downloads run; nothing is modal. ⋯ › Downloads lists them (Queued with pause and remove, Done with the storage used, Different Voice), each with its voice; after a voice change a download offers *Re-download* or *Keep*. |
| P9 | A voice model download (Phonon ~131 MB, Kokoro ~163 MB) starts at once on Wi-Fi. On cellular or with Low Data Mode on it asks first ("Download on Cellular?": *Download* or *Wait for Wi-Fi*); waiting cancels that download for now. Apple voices never ask. |

## Retention and Prepared Audio (C134, C135, C136)

- **Three states.** *Prepared*: saved and you chose *Prepare Audio*; its audio is never cleared automatically. *Saved*: in the Library; listening renders audio as it goes, and saving or starring no longer renders the whole article. *Being read*: opened or played without saving, listed in Recent.
- **Recent is the last 50 articles started.** At launch and when an article starts, unsaved articles beyond those 50 are deleted with their audio; saved articles beyond them lose only their audio from listening. Audio the session or the staged chapter needs is never touched. The finished-chapters trim skips prepared audio.
- **Prepared Audio list** (⋯ › Prepared Audio, Settings): the prepared sections (Now, Waiting, Prepared, Different Voice) and **From Listening** (audio rendered while listening, no ↓, with a footer about Recent 50); every row names its voice (C138). Every row has *Delete Audio* (swipe and ⋯), which never deletes the article. The list's ⋯ (`MenuRegistry`, `.deleteAllPrepared`, `.clearListeningAudio`) has **Delete All…** ("Delete N articles and their prepared audio? This can't be undone.": deletes every prepared article and its audio, folders stay) and **Clear Audio From Listening…** (that audio only), each shown only when its section has rows.
- **Naming.** The feature is *Prepare*, in the UI and in code (`PrepareCenter`, `PrepareRecord`, `PreparedAudioSheet`, `Features/PreparedAudio`). *Download* stays only for real file downloads (voice models, the CPU model, EPUB files). On-disk names are kept: `Downloads.json`, its keys, `downloads.wordsPerSecond`.
