# Functional requirements

Meridian Reader is a personal tool for **listening to web articles**: find a page in the in-app
browser, get a clean copy, and listen to it (or read it) on the device, offline once saved. How
the screens look and move: `NAVIGATION.md`. How it is built: `TECHNICAL_SPEC.md`.

## Surfaces

| Surface | Role |
|--------|------|
| **Library** | The folder tree: the home is the top level, with Everything (its folders, books and series among them, then the pages in no folder, pages played without saving on top, in the top level's sort) and Favorites (starred folders and pages from anywhere) tabs, then folder → folder at any depth. The same bottom band as the browser (D22, D23): the Now Playing bar across the top, the swap then the search field, then ‹ › ＋ 🕘 ⋯ in the browser's ‹ › 🔖 🕘 ⋯ slots. No tab bar. |
| **Browser** | A start page (Favorites, Recent Searches) or a web page, with one bottom bar (address field + toolbar). |
| **Reader** | A sheet for reading any page, over the browser or the Library, with its own Listen. |
| **Now Playing** | A sheet over everything for what you hear, in the Reader's reading theme and type: a title card, the text following the audio (the spoken paragraph highlighted, every paragraph in full ink), and the player's controls. |
| **Settings** | ⋯ › Settings, the last row of every ⋯ panel: Appearance (System / Light / Dark, five app colours), Reading (theme, font, size, spacing, Scroll / Pages; the same as Aa), Listening (engine, voice, language, speed), Browsing (Block Ads), Downloads & Storage, and Developer (developer options only). |
| **Now Playing bar** | What you hear, on the browser, the start page, every Library screen and the Reader sheet, from the first play until *Stop Listening* (long-press: the page's menu, then *Stop Listening*) or the page is removed from the Library (its menu's *Remove from Library*, or anywhere else), deleted, or revised by a book's *Replace*; it keeps a finished page with ▶. Tap or swipe up → Now Playing. |

The app reopens on the surface you left: the Library at the folder you were in, or the browser on
its last page (reloaded). A fresh install opens the Library.

## Browser

| ID | Requirement |
|----|-------------|
| B1 | The address field takes a URL (loaded in the in-app web view) or search text (a Google search). |
| B2 | Pages open only by user action; links navigate normally. ‹ / › are web history. |
| B3 | The start page shows starred pages and folders as a Favorites icon grid, along with Recent Searches; Show All opens the Library's Favorites tab. |
| B4 | While typing: local matches (history, saved articles) first, then live Google suggestions (unofficial endpoint, debounced). If Google fails, only local matches show, with no error. |
| B5 | History keeps the last 30 visited pages (title, host, favicon), deduped by canonical URL. Clearing history never deletes saved articles. |
| B6 | ☆ *Add to Favorites* sets an independent star flag without moving the page. A new favorite may be a link (URL, title, favicon) without being saved; a page stays where it is, in the Library or not (starring never files it). Favorites lists every starred page, in the Library or not, and a starred page is never pruned. Unstarring only clears the star. *Remove from Library* never clears it: the page leaves every Library list and stays in Favorites. One page per address; a chapter can be starred like any other page. |
| B7 | Dictation fills the address field while searching, on-device where the language allows; nothing is submitted automatically, and listening pauses while the mic is open. |
| B8 | Loading pages needs the network. |
| B9 | A readable page shows 🎧 at the end of the address field: grey when it isn't what you hear (tap: listen now, replacing what plays once this page is extracted; "Nothing to listen to on this page" when it has too little text), accent with bars when it is (tap: pause / resume). |
| B10 | Video and audio on a page play only after a tap. *Block Ads* (Settings › Browsing, on by default) keeps requests to common ad and tracker hosts from loading, in every web view the app makes; a link through an ad host (a sponsored search result) doesn't open; a change applies to pages loaded after it. |

## Reader

| ID | Requirement |
|----|-------------|
| R1 | Reader mode is manual: the address field's reader icon or ⋯ → Reader, only on pages that look readable, or a Favorites tile or a Library row. It never turns on by itself. Opening the Reader of the page you hear opens Now Playing instead. |
| R2 | The Reader shows the cleaned article on paper in the serif reading style, under its title and source. A page has a title only when its source gives one (the page's own, a book's contents entry or heading, a story's chapter link); without one, the Reader and Now Playing show none, and rows, the bar and the lock screen name the page by its site (a chapter by its book). Nothing makes a title up. A page part-way through opens with the sentence at its saved listening spot in the middle, underlined; after that the text never scrolls by itself. |
| R3 | Its Listen ("Listen" with "9 min", "Continue" with "6 min left", "Listen Again") starts the page at its saved spot and turns the Reader into Now Playing; ☝︎ then a tap on a paragraph listens from there. The Reader has no speed or skip buttons. |
| R4 | Save from the Reader or Now Playing keeps the screen and playback as they are; *Remove from Library* there removes as a Library row does (S6), so the page you hear stops and Now Playing closes. 🔖's long-press saves the page into a folder picked in the Move tree. |
| R5 | The Reader's back pill ("‹ Browser", "‹ Library"), its grabber and a pull down (on the header, or on the text from its top, with the bars showing) close it to exactly where you were. Closing never stops audio. |
| R6 | Now Playing follows the audio: it opens with the spoken sentence near the middle and keeps it there, the spoken paragraph on the theme's highlight (every paragraph in full ink, C105), the sentence underlined in the theme's accent; scrolling stops following and offers *Jump to Audio* (with the bars up only, never in full screen, C128), which brings the spoken sentence back to the middle. Controls: play / pause, previous / next paragraph, speed (0.75×–2×), a sentence-precise scrubber with fine scrubbing, Aa, ☝︎. It shares the Reader's top bar, 🔖, ⋯ and reading appearance (D24): its close pill says where it plays from, ⋯ → *Go to* opens that place, 🔖 saves or files. At the end it stays, with "◉ Finished" and ▶. The close pill or a pull down (on the header, or on the text from its top) shrinks it into the bar. |
| R7 | The Reader and Now Playing read full screen: scrolling the text down hides the top bar, the bottom band and the status bar; only a tap in the middle of the text brings them back (scrolling up doesn't, and a touch that stops or ends a scroll is never a tap, C105) over the text, which never moves for either. A tap at the right or left edge of the text turns a screen (the left edge's first 48 pt excepted); in full screen a small play / pause floats at the bottom right for 3 s after it begins and after each page turn. At the top in full screen a pull down brings the bars back; the next one closes. Links in the text open in the browser. |
| R8 | With Pages chosen (Aa or Settings › Reading), the Reader and Now Playing show the text a page of whole lines at a time, read full screen: each page fills the screen inside the safe area with an even margin above and below (C113); a tap in the middle shows the bars and shrinks the same page, unreflowed, into an accent-outlined card between them, its neighbours separate cards beside it parted by a gap and outlined in the theme's rule grey (C116), the accent following the centred card as they swipe, which swipe sideways, and a tap on the card zooms back (a spring, a fade under Reduce Motion), with the time left from the page's first character on the left of a footer and how far through (%) on the right, shown while reading. Pages turn with native horizontal paging (C107): the page tracks the finger and settles with the system's physics, an edge tap slides one page (the left edge's first 48 pt stay the system's back swipe); Pages never follow the audio and nothing floats over their text: *Jump to Audio* shows above the band, as in Scroll, while the bars show and the voice is on another page (C108, C111); a new layout or a switch between Scroll and Pages keeps the page holding the line being read. |

## Save and the Library

| ID | Requirement |
|----|-------------|
| S1 | Save is available from the browser (⋯ → *Save to Folder…*, into a folder picked in the Move tree) and the Reader, and always runs extraction. |
| S2 | On success the full article is stored on the device (text, cleaned HTML, metadata). |
| S3 | On failure Save is blocked with the reason, and nothing is stored. |
| S4 | One page per canonical URL: saving, adding to Favorites or listening to the same page twice (tracking parameters, fragments) keeps one page, in at most one folder. |
| S5 | Folders nest to any depth. A folder lists its subfolders, then its pages by its sort (Manual, Newest, Title); Edit selects rows to move, mark finished or remove, and drags them when Manual. Without Edit, a row lifted with a long press is filed by dropping it on a folder, or placed by dropping it between rows (which makes the folder's sort Manual); a folder can't go into itself or a folder inside it. Books and series are folders like any other: they take drops and subfolders, and their chapters move, drag and reorder as any page. Folders can be made, renamed, moved and deleted: deleting offers only *Delete Folder and Contents*, which deletes everything inside with its audio. A delete that can't be saved leaves the pages and their audio. The home's Favorites tab and search results gather rows from across the tree: they don't drag, and a folder there opens under its parents. |
| S6 | Rows show a length, "N min left" part-way or "Finished", with the listening mark in a right-aligned column before ⋯ (a ring part-way, the same ring full when finished, no ✓; bars while playing) (folders: what is in them, or their place; every length and time left in the app counts at the playback speed), a small accent filled ↓ when downloaded by a download (C89, C97) (in the current voice, or one kept; listening alone never marks it), the download state in one place, the small mark before the second line (a spinner queued, the accent ↓ filling while it downloads, filled when done; the thumbnail never changes, C102), and the playing row is tinted. A tap plays a page row (Now Playing for what you hear, the browser for a link) and opens a folder row; the row's only control is ⋯, and a long-press opens the same menu: the item's one menu, the same rows in the same order wherever the page or folder shows (rows, Favorites tiles, History visits, the Now Playing bar, a folder's header and screen, the Reader, Now Playing, the browser; NAVIGATION.md → "Menus"); swipe left is its *Remove from Library* (*Remove from Recent*, a folder's *Delete Folder…*). Removing a page, from here or anywhere else (Edit, 🔖, the ⋯ of the browser, the Reader and Now Playing, the Now Playing bar's long-press), is one action: it takes the page out of every list it was in (Pages, its folder, search, Downloads) and keeps its star (Favorites lists only saved pages, so it shows there again once saved, C104), and it doesn't come back in Recent (saving it, or listening to it again with 🎧 or the Reader's Listen, does); the page you hear stops, and the Now Playing bar offers the page played before it, if one is still listed; a remove that can't be saved changes nothing and says so. |
| S7 | Nothing on the Library home appears or goes away because playback starts or ends. The row of any folder whose pages play (a book or series too) shows its place (the page it is at) and time left ("6 h 10 min left"), and its ⋯ › *Continue* resumes it there without opening it; a part-way page's row shows the time left and a tap resumes it; the Now Playing bar's ▶ resumes what you last heard. Pages played without saving are in the home's Recent tab (not Everything), with *Save to Library* and *Remove from Recent* in their menu; search covers the tab on screen (on a folder's screen, everything under that folder). |
| S8 | A book or series is a folder: it opens to the folder screen with its details (cover, author), a Continue / Play from Start button, and its chapters, first in the source's order (front matter skipped in playback, *Skip in Playback* per chapter); after that its order, sort and contents are the user's, as for any folder. Playing any chapter carries on through the book in its order, then stops. |
| S11 | Any other folder with pages that play plays like a book: its screen has the same Continue / Play from Start button, and playing any of its pages carries on through its pages in its order (its sort), page to page, passing over link pages, then stops. The page that last played is its place. A page in no folder plays to its end and stops. Now Playing says what it plays from: the folder, else the Library or the site. |
| S9 | Search in the Library covers titles, sites, folder names and authors across the tree (begun on a folder's screen, under that folder, and the field names it), and offers *Search the web*. |
| S10 | *Import EPUB…* (the Library's ＋ and ⋯, the empty Library) imports a DRM-free EPUB picked in Files, or one an `.epub` link in the browser downloads (asked first), as a book: one folder with one page per chapter, front matter skipped in playback. The import shows its progress, can be cancelled (keeping nothing), and files the book in *Add to* (the folder it started from, else the Library), which can change while it runs; it then opens the book. One import runs at a time. Copy-protected, fixed-layout, too-large and unreadable files are refused with a one-line reason. A book already in the Library asks *Replace* (same folder, its order kept, places kept where chapters match; chapters moved elsewhere are found and updated where they are, new ones go after the chapter before them) or *Cancel*. |
| S12 | *Follow…* (the browser's ⋯ on a story's page, the Library's ⋯ with a typed address) reads the story's table of contents (Royal Road's story page, or any site's longest list of chapter links) into Review: every chapter with a box, *Select All*, and a preview of a chapter on tapping its row. OK makes the story's folder at once with the chapters kept, as links, whose text is then fetched while the app is open, strictly one at a time with a pause, backing off when the site asks to slow down or doesn't answer (a notice in the folder's header names the chapter that waits, by its title), with one status line in the folder's header, which also counts chapters that couldn't load. ▶ on a chapter still waiting fetches it first, then plays it; playing through the story stops before a chapter not fetched yet. About once a day while the app is in use the contents are read again and new chapters go at the end (left-out ones stay out). The Following sheet shows the last and next check and the fetch progress, with *Check Now* and *Unfollow* (the chapters stay). No feeds, no notifications. |

## Listening

| ID | Requirement |
|----|-------------|
| P1 | Apple TTS is the default and always available. Kokoro (on-device, English, A15+) is optional: downloaded on selection, deletable, and used only once ready. Apple speaks whenever Kokoro can't (not downloaded, loading, failed, another language, a chunk it can't render live), in the best installed Apple voice for the language (Premium, then Enhanced, then default; the Kokoro voice's gender first). C129: a Kokoro crash never switches engine or voice: the same voice resumes on ONNX CPU silently (one thread fewer each time); after a second crash in a row downloads and new rendering pause, playback goes on through audio already rendered, and only where it reaches audio not rendered yet does it pause there with a plain banner and Retry (Retry restarts Kokoro and resumes rendering and downloads). Apple audio is never written to the download cache. An app install that cuts a call off isn't counted as a crash. |
| P2 | Playback never goes silent or freezes mid-article: anything the selected engine can't produce is spoken with Apple. |
| P3 | Each article keeps one voice's audio; switching voice re-renders it, and the old voice plays until each paragraph is replaced. |
| P4 | The position is remembered per article and resumed later, for an article listened to without saving too (kept 7 days after it was last played); after a Kokoro crash the app reopens the article at the paragraph that was playing, or at its own spot, paused, when its text has changed since. |
| P5 | Listening continues in the background, with lock-screen / Control Center controls (play, pause, next / previous paragraph, rate). Calls and unplugged headphones pause; a resumable interruption resumes. |
| P6 | The article's language is detected; an article the selected engine can't speak uses an Apple voice for its language, without changing settings. |
| P7 | Saved articles play fully offline. |
| P8 | *Download* on a page, folder, book or followed story fetches its text (keeping a link page's in place) and renders its whole audio in the on-device voice ahead of time, one page at a time, behind what is playing, while the app is in front and, on the ONNX CPU route, in the background while audio plays (the playing page always first). A folder's Download is a toggle (its header's Download control, and one *Download* / *Turn Off Download* row in its menus): while on, every page added to it later downloads too; turned off, what is downloaded stays; turned back on over a folder all downloaded, nothing is downloaded again. A slim line on the bottom chrome shows progress while downloads run; nothing is modal. ⋯ › Downloads lists them (Queued with pause and remove, Done with the storage used, Different Voice), each with its voice; after a voice change a download offers *Re-download* or *Keep*. |

## Not in the app

- Accounts, a backend, sync.
- Tabs; a share extension or open-in as an entry point.
- Auto reader mode, auto-opening links, saving failed extractions.
- Cloud voices.
- Tags, highlights.

| W1 | Item wording (C120): items are *articles* in every count, empty state and message ("3 articles"); in a folder imported from an EPUB they are *chapters*. Never "pages" for items. The Recent tab lists only started articles and chapters, never folders, and ＋ on the Library home has *New Folder…* (C119). *New Folder…* is the one name everywhere: ＋, a folder header's More, Edit's bar (which files the selection into it) and Move to…; it is hidden in an imported EPUB or followed story (C122). One create group, the same everywhere (＋ on the home and in a folder, a folder's More, Edit's ＋): *New Folder…*, *Import from…* (an EPUB), *Save from Website…* (an address, saved into the folder on screen), *Paste Text…* (a sheet with an optional title and the text, prefilled from the clipboard; *Save* makes a new article of it here, titled by its first sentence if no title is typed; it reads, plays and downloads like any article, C127), *Follow…*; hidden whole in an imported EPUB or followed story (C126). |
